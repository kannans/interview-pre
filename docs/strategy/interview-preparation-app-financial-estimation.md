# Interview Preparation Coach Financial Estimation

## Document Purpose

This document gives a founder-friendly sample framework for estimating:

- infrastructure cost
- CAPEX
- OPEX
- revenue
- contribution margin
- break-even direction

It is not an accounting statement. It is an early startup planning model for the focused version of the product:

`AI interview recovery coach for experienced engineers`

## Important Note

All numbers below are sample planning estimates, not final market quotes.

The purpose of this document is to help answer:

- how to think about costs
- what should go into a pitch deck
- how revenue and cost may scale
- what metrics matter before building

## 1. Financial Terms in Simple Words

### CAPEX

One-time setup or initial investment cost.

Examples:

- product design
- MVP development
- branding
- domain and legal setup
- initial content bank creation

### OPEX

Monthly operating cost to run the product.

Examples:

- hosting
- database
- AI usage
- storage
- email and analytics tools
- maintenance
- customer support
- marketing

### Revenue

Money coming in from customers.

Examples:

- monthly subscriptions
- premium sprint packs
- expert mock interview add-ons

## 2. What Matters Most for This Product

For this app, the most important financial drivers are:

- AI cost per active user
- number of active users
- paid conversion rate
- average revenue per paying user
- retention
- monthly marketing spend

This type of business usually has low CAPEX and meaningful ongoing OPEX.

## 6. Revenue Formula

Basic subscription revenue formula:

`Monthly Revenue = Paying Users x Average Revenue Per Paying User`

Example:

- 100 paying users
- average revenue per paying user = 1,800 INR

Then:

`Monthly Revenue = 100 x 1,800 = 180,000 INR`

## 9. What This Tells You

The model above shows a common early-stage reality:

- early months may run at a loss
- AI cost and marketing can eat margin
- scale alone does not guarantee profit

You need either:

- higher pricing
- better conversion
- stronger retention
- lower AI cost per session
- upsell products with better margins

## 10. Better Revenue Case with Upsells

This business becomes stronger when you add high-value offers.

Example monthly upside:

- 120 paying users at 1,800 INR average = 216,000 INR
- 20 sprint pack sales at 4,999 INR = 99,980 INR
- 10 expert mock sessions at 2,999 INR = 29,990 INR

Then:

`Total Monthly Revenue = 216,000 + 99,980 + 29,990 = 345,970 INR`

If monthly OPEX is 255,000 INR:

`Estimated Profit = 90,970 INR`

That is why subscriptions alone may not be enough early on. Premium outcome-focused offers can materially improve the business.

## 11. Infrastructure Cost Estimation Method

To estimate infra more realistically later, use this formula:

`Total Monthly Infra Cost = Fixed Infra Cost + (Active Users x Avg Usage Cost per User)`

### Fixed Infra Cost

- hosting
- database base plan
- auth tools
- analytics
- monitoring

### Variable Usage Cost per User

- AI sessions
- storage growth
- resume processing
- interview report generation

## 12. Simple Per-User AI Cost Model

You can track AI cost using this formula:

`AI Cost per User per Month = Number of Sessions x Average Cost per Session`

Example:

- 12 sessions per active user per month
- 12 INR average AI cost per session

Then:

`AI Cost per Active User per Month = 144 INR`

If you have 400 active users:

`Monthly AI Cost = 400 x 144 = 57,600 INR`

This is why session design, prompt efficiency, and model selection matter a lot.

## 13. Break-Even Thinking

Basic formula:

`Break-Even Users = Total Monthly OPEX / Average Monthly Gross Profit per Paying User`

Example:

- Monthly OPEX = 255,000 INR
- average revenue per paying user = 1,800 INR
- average direct variable cost per paying user = 300 INR
- gross profit per paying user = 1,500 INR

Then:

`Break-Even Paying Users = 255,000 / 1,500 = 170`

So in this example, you need around `170 paying users` to cover monthly operating cost.

## 14. What Should Go into the Pitch Deck?

Yes, this is part of a pitch deck.

For an early-stage pitch deck, include:

- business model
- pricing strategy
- revenue streams
- high-level cost structure
- estimated monthly burn
- break-even logic
- 12-month growth assumptions

You do not need audit-level finance details in an early deck. You do need believable assumptions.

## 17. Founder Recommendation

If you launch this product, start with a lean financial model.

Do not assume massive scale in the beginning.

Instead:

- keep infrastructure simple
- control AI usage carefully
- use a focused niche
- charge for high-intent use cases
- add premium upsells early

That approach gives you a much better chance of reaching sustainable unit economics.

## 18. Final Summary

For this product, the biggest business question is not whether people will use AI features. The biggest question is whether:

- users will pay for interview recovery
- AI cost per user stays under control
- conversion and upsells are strong enough to create margin

This is why an early financial model should focus on:

- one-time CAPEX
- monthly OPEX
- revenue by scenario
- break-even users
- pricing and margin assumptions
